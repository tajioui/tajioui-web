STAMM, the pre-ordering and Apple Wallet stamp card product InfraMS builds for independent cafés, lets guests order ahead and pay at the counter. That raises one question before any other: what stops people from ordering and never coming, or from ordering on purpose to waste a barista's time? This paper collects what payment pricing, German law, chain practice, fraud engineering and behavioural research say about it, and ends with a ranked design.

The cheapest strong defence against no-shows and fake orders needs no payment at all. **Do not start a made-to-order drink or bowl until the guest signals arrival, and only let a card pre-order after a barista has stamped it at the till.** McDonald's Germany already runs the first half of this. It charges and cooks nothing until the guest is near the restaurant, and an uncollected order expires unpaid after a day ([McDonald's DE App-FAQ](https://www.mcdonalds.com/de/de-de/app/faq-app.html)). Add a few simple rules (one open order per card, a value cap, one no-show pauses pre-ordering until the next till stamp) and a remote troll cannot make the café produce a single drink, while regulars barely notice. Per-order card payment is what the chains do, but at Stripe it takes **0.31 € (7.8 %) of a 4 € coffee** ([Stripe pricing DE](https://stripe.com/de/pricing)). The best within-event data also suggest that most of its effect comes from *who* chooses to pay rather than from paying itself ([PheedLoop #06](https://pheedloop.com/blog/event-data-lab-report-06)). A no-show fee without money taken up front cannot be collected in practice, because a Mahnbescheid alone costs at least 38 € ([GKG Anlage 1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gkg_2004/anlage_1.html)). Real geofencing is impossible in a web app ([W3C Geolocation](https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/)), so a tap, or a code shown only at the counter, takes its place. If STAMM later wants money in the loop, the café must be the merchant of record, through Stripe Connect direct charges or a top-up balance the café issues itself. If the platform collects guests' money and pays cafés out, that is an unlicensed payment service, punishable by up to five years in prison ([§ 63 ZAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/zag_2018/__63.html)). Nobody publishes a no-show rate for café order-ahead. The analogues run from 1.30 % for German restaurant reservations to about 28 % for free events, so a pilot has to log every "nicht abgeholt" from day one.

Items marked `unverified` come from search snippets, secondary sources or assumptions, and should not be repeated as fact.*

## 1. No café publishes a no-show rate, and the analogues span 1 % to 28 %

No earnings call, investor dashboard or report from Starbucks, LAP, McDonald's, Ritual or Too Good To Go gives a no-show or abandonment rate for coffee or quick-service order-ahead, prepaid or not. Every number in this section is an analogue. The largest dataset is the vendor Resos's No-Show Index: **3,768,761 reservations at 2,417 restaurants (data 01.08.2025 to 31.07.2026), with a recorded no-show rate of 2.33 % overall and 1.30 % in Germany** (58,523 reservations, 51 restaurants). Resos says the recorded figure likely understates the problem ([Resos](https://resos.com/restaurant-no-show-rate/); [GCN, 24.10.2026](https://gcn.com/restaurants-deposits-no-show-reservations/22729)). Bloom Intelligence, another vendor, measured **3.7 %** across about 50 locations (data 01.01.2025 to 06.08.2026). Online bookings no-showed at 3.9 % against 3.0 % for phone bookings, and parties of one or two at 4.5 % ([Bloom Intelligence](https://bloomintelligence.com/blog/restaurant-no-shows/)). The German hospitality association has prevalence figures but no rate. In a DEHOGA NRW survey of 250 operators (article 04.12.2025, survey date not stated), **87 % knew no-shows from their own experience**, about one in ten demanded a deposit or a chargeable card, and 3 % had stopped taking reservations ([tageskarte.io](https://www.tageskarte.io/gastronomie/detail/87-prozent-betroffen-das-no-show-dilemma-der-restaurants.html)).

The closest pay-at-collection analogue is US pharmacy pickup, where the patient pays at the counter. **3.27 % of 10.35 million prescriptions were abandoned (2008 data), falling to 1.4 % when the copay was 10 USD or less** and rising to 4.5 % above 50 USD. The study was funded by CVS Caremark and was read only through an index summary ([QxMD](https://read.qxmd.com/read/21079218/the-epidemiology-of-prescriptions-abandoned-at-the-pharmacy)). Prepayment alone does not guarantee pickup. About one in seven UK click-and-collect shoppers failed to collect, worth £228m a year (Barclaycard research, Retail Week 20.08.2019), because retailers refund uncollected goods ([Retail Week](https://www.retail-week.com/data/brits-fail-to-pick-up-228m-of-click-and-collect-orders/7032709.article)). Free commitments behave much worse. PheedLoop's event check-in data show a **median no-show of about 28 % for free events against about 17 % for paid ones** (389 and 683 events; publication date not checked) ([PheedLoop #05](https://pheedloop.com/blog/event-data-lab-report-5)). Indian vendor blogs (2025 to 2026, unaudited) put cash-on-delivery returns at 3 to 7 times the prepaid rate ([Amazon Shipping India](https://shipping.amazon.in/blog/what-is-rto-how-to-reduce-return-to-origin)).

A STAMM pre-order is a one-person, online, zero-cost commitment, which is the highest-risk profile in the Bloom data. It also has a very short lead time, and same-day restaurant bookings are among the most reliable in the Resos data at 2.09 % ([Resos](https://resos.com/restaurant-no-show-rate/)). A low single-digit rate at a pilot café is plausible but `unverified`. Nobody knows the real figure, and the pilot is the first dataset that can supply it.

Deliberate fake orders are documented, but the sources show them rarely. In Cincinnati (04.2021), a restaurant lost 350 USD to four prank orders in one week. The callers promised to pay cash at pickup and never came, and the restaurant switched to taking payment before cooking ([FOX19, 06.04.2021](https://www.fox19.com/2021/04/06/clifton-chinese-restaurant-forced-change-operations-after-prank-orders/?outputType=amp)). In Germany, mass fake Lieferando orders sent to streamers ("Essensraids") left restaurants with the cost. The advocacy group TeamKompass puts the damage in the double-digit millions, an estimate MeinMMO did not check (`unverified`) ([MeinMMO, 15.03.2024](https://mein-mmo.de/en/essensraids-problem-auf-twitch,1103034)). No documented case of competitor sabotage at a café was found. According to baristas, the most common cause of uncollected Starbucks mobile orders is guests ordering to the wrong store (anecdotal) ([The Takeout, 28.04.2022](https://thetakeout.com/how-long-will-starbucks-wait-before-tossing-your-mobile-1848853987)). That matters for STAMM as it adds cafés: the café's name and address must be unmissable at the order button and on the Wallet order.

## 2. Big operators either take the money or cook on arrival

Every large order-ahead system checked in Germany takes money in the app, and none runs pay-at-pickup with food made in advance.

| Operator | Money up front? | Uncollected order | Abuse rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| LAP Coffee, DE incl. Frankfurt ([terms as of 04.2026](https://lap.coffee/terms-and-conditions); [FAQ](https://www.lap.coffee/faq)) | Yes: card, wallet, PayPal or a prepaid LAP Card | LAP may withdraw and claim "any resulting costs"; no hold time published | May reject an order if the user is "unlikely to be at the Location"; may suspend stamp collection |
| Starbucks DE ([terms valid from 16.05.2026](https://www.starbucks.de/sites/starbucks-de-pwa/files/2026-03/Nutzungsbedingungen_MOP_31032026.pdf)) | Yes, due at order | Pickup recommended within 15 min; binned at closing, no refund | May refuse the service for "Nichtabholung ... ohne berechtigte Gründe", including repeat cases |
| McDonald's DE ([App-FAQ](https://www.mcdonalds.com/de/de-de/app/faq-app.html)) | Card on file, charged only at check-in near the restaurant | Not cooked until check-in; expires after a day, nothing charged | The design removes the payoff |
| McDonald's Malta ([FAQ](https://mcdonalds.com.mt/mobile-order-pay/faq/)) | Yes | Binned after 20 min, no refund | Short hold |
| Ritual ([terms](https://ritual.co/terms)) | Yes, "non-refundable" | No cancel, no refund | May restrict access without notice |
| OpenTable ([terms](https://www.opentable.com/c/?p=18768)) | The restaurant may require a card | Card charged under the restaurant's policy | Account suspended after 4 no-shows in 12 months |
| TheFork ([CEO op-ed, 06.08.2021](https://www.hospitalitynewsmag.com/handling-no-shows-at-restaurants/)) | Card imprint optional | n/a | Warning, then suspension; staff see a per-guest reliability meter |

The sources split into two designs. The first is **prepay and forfeit**, used by Starbucks DE, LAP, Ritual and McDonald's Malta: the guest pays, and an uncollected order is binned without a refund. The second is **fire on arrival**, used by McDonald's Germany and New Zealand (and Australia per a search snippet, `unverified`): nothing is cooked until the guest checks in nearby, and in Germany nothing is charged either. Pay-at-pickup survives only in the long tail. Toast has it as a restaurant setting ([Toast docs](https://doc.toasttab.com/doc/devguide/apiRestaurantOnlineOrderingInfo.html)), the app of the German bakery café Cafe Zürn lets guests pay "wie gewohnt an der Kasse" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/de/app/cafe-z%C3%BCrn/id1559678706)), and Square declined to build it, citing "a higher level of risk to businesses" (community staff answer, around 2020) ([Square community](https://community.squareup.com/t5/Online-Store/After-placing-an-Square-Online-order-can-my-customers-pay-in/m-p/354884)). None of them publishes no-show figures. Even the prepaid chains keep discretionary clauses to refuse service or reject "inauthentic" orders, which shows that payment does not end non-collection or fake orders on its own.

The reservation platforms are the closest analogue to STAMM's free order backed by an email identity. OpenTable publishes a hard rule of suspension after four no-shows in twelve months. TheFork warns repeat offenders first, then suspends. In its CEO's op-ed (vendor, 06.08.2021), TheFork says this cut no-shows by 31 % and that **90 % of no-showers did not repeat within six months**, with no baseline given ([Hospitality News](https://www.hospitalitynewsmag.com/handling-no-shows-at-restaurants/)). Money also changes behaviour, but the figures come from vendors who sell deposits. OpenTable claims a 60 % reduction for restaurants using its deposits (vendor claim, reported 06.10.2026) ([Restaurant Online](https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2026/10/06/no-show-rates-fall-year-on-year-report-finds/)). Resos records 0.80 % no-shows with a deposit or card hold against 2.47 % without, but says itself that the comparison cannot separate the feature from the kind of restaurant that uses it ([Resos](https://resos.com/restaurant-no-show-rate/)). Tock reports 0.9 % for prepaid bookings, 1.7 % with deposits and 3 % with card holds (12.2023 to 03.2024, snippet-level, `unverified`) ([Tock](https://exploretock.com/join/resources/eliminate-no-shows-3-tock-tools/)). Guarantees cost bookings too. Quandoo's UK manager said in 11.2024 that deposits and card capture "can deter" diners ([Hospitality & Catering News](https://www.hospitalityandcateringnews.com/2024/11/restaurants-are-adapting-reservation-strategies-to-match-changing-dining-out-trends/)), and one secondary source puts the loss at 10 to 15 % of booking completions (`unverified`) ([GCN](https://gcn.com/restaurants-deposits-no-show-reservations/22729)).

Platforms that do offer pay-later treat it as a privilege that has to be earned. eBay gives every new seller a limit that "rises as you meet buyer demand" ([eBay](https://export.ebay.com/in/first-steps/rules-and-restrictions-in-selling/selling-limits/)). Uber Eats carries an unpaid cash balance on the account into the next order ([Uber cash terms](https://www.uber.com/us/en/legal/consumer-cash-payment-terms/)). Zomato riders reported in 2023 that after a cancellation the app sometimes bars cash-on-delivery for the customer's next few orders; this comes from rider accounts, not an official policy ([BOOM, 24.01.2023](https://www.boomlive.in/boom-reports/zomato-food-delivery-cod-scam-ceo-deepinder-goyal-20831)). DoorDash keeps cash out of its main app entirely ([DoorDash newsroom](https://doordash.news/merchant/introducing-cash-on-delivery-enabling-restaurateurs-to-accept-cash-orders-via-doordash-drive/)). STAMM can borrow three pieces from these models: the timing from McDonald's, the warn-then-suspend ladder from OpenTable and TheFork, and the earned limit from eBay.

## 3. Card payment costs 7.8 % of a coffee, and every workaround has a catch

Every mainstream online rail charges a fixed fee per transaction (0.25 € at Stripe and Mollie, 0.39 € at PayPal), and that fee sets the cost floor on a small café ticket.

| Rail | 4 € coffee | 12 € bowl | 20 € top-up | 50 € top-up | 1 € deposit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stripe card, EEA standard (Apple Pay and Google Pay cost the same) | 0.31 € (7.8 %) | 0.43 € (3.6 %) | 0.55 € (2.8 %) | 1.00 € (2.0 %) | 0.27 € (26.5 %) |
| PayPal Checkout DE (fee PDF dated 04.11.2026) | 0.51 € (12.7 %) | 0.75 € (6.2 %) | 0.99 € (4.9 %) | 1.89 € (3.8 %) | 0.42 € (42 %) |
| Mollie Wero, rate for DE merchants | 0.29 € (7.1 %) | 0.36 € (3.0 %) | 0.43 € (2.1 %) | 0.70 € (1.4 %) | 0.26 € (25.9 %) |
| SEPA Lastschrift (Stripe or Mollie) | 0.35 € (8.8 %) | 0.35 € (2.9 %) | 0.35 € (1.7 %) | 0.35 € (0.7 %) | 0.35 € (35 %) |
| SumUp online 2.5 % (non-API rate; API pricing set by sales) | 0.10 € (2.5 %) | 0.30 € (2.5 %) | 0.50 € (2.5 %) | 1.25 € (2.5 %) | 0.03 € (2.5 %) |
| Adyen card, 0.11 € + 0.60 % + interchange (scheme fees `unverified`) | about 0.14-0.15 € | about 0.21-0.22 € | about 0.27-0.29 € | about 0.51-0.56 € | about 0.12 € |

*Computed from the providers' own pricing pages: [Stripe](https://stripe.com/de/pricing), [Stripe local methods](https://stripe.com/de/pricing/local-payment-methods), [PayPal fee PDF](https://www.paypalobjects.com/marketing/ua/pdf/DE/de/de-de-merchant-fees-04-Nov-2026.pdf), [Mollie](https://www.mollie.com/de/pricing), [SumUp](https://www.sumup.com/de-de/online-zahlungen/), [Adyen](https://www.adyen.com/de_DE/preise).*

**Per-order prepayment.** The fee objection holds up. Stripe charges 1.5 % + 0.25 € on standard EEA cards, and refunds do not return the fee ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/understanding-fees-for-refunded-payments)). PayPal keeps its 0.39 € fixed fee on every refund ([PayPal fee PDF](https://www.paypalobjects.com/marketing/ua/pdf/DE/de/de-de-merchant-fees-04-Nov-2026.pdf)). SumUp's pure 2.5 % is the only rate without a fixed fee and beats Stripe below 25 €. But SumUp is a per-merchant product, and its price for an API integration like STAMM's is negotiated with sales, so the 2.5 % is not guaranteed ([SumUp](https://www.sumup.com/de-de/online-zahlungen/)). Adyen is cheapest on paper, but it does not publish its minimum invoice amount, so whether it would take a single small café is `unverified` ([Adyen](https://www.adyen.com/de_DE/preise)). To be fair to payment: when the guest would have paid by card at the counter anyway, the café already pays a card-present fee there (SumUp 0.79 % to 1.39 %, [SumUp Preise](https://www.sumup.com/de-de/preise/)), so the real extra cost of moving that payment online is about 0.25 to 0.28 € per coffee (computed). That is still 6 to 7 % of the price.

**Card holds.** Stripe, Mollie and Adyen can authorise a card at order and capture later. At Stripe, Visa, Mastercard and Amex online authorisations last 7 days, and partial capture is supported ([Stripe hold docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/place-a-hold-on-a-payment-method)). Capturing at pickup costs the same as prepaying. Holding and then releasing at pickup while the guest pays at the counter would cost something only on no-shows, *if* uncaptured authorisations are free, and no pricing page confirms that (`unverified`). Stripe also warns that some banks show a hold like a charge, so the guest may think they paid twice. Capturing only on a no-show is technically possible, but the economics favour the attacker. Capturing a 4 € no-show nets 3.69 €, while a single dispute costs 20 € at Stripe or a 16 € chargeback fee at PayPal ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/de/pricing); [PayPal fee PDF](https://www.paypalobjects.com/marketing/ua/pdf/DE/de/de-de-merchant-fees-04-Nov-2026.pdf)). A saboteur using a stolen card generates exactly those disputes, so holds deter careless guests, not saboteurs. Stripe further warns that card networks may restrict 1-USD authorisations that are never meant to be captured, so any hold should be for the real order amount. **Open legal question:** a no-show capture is cleanest when the authorised amount equals the exact order total and the terms say the price is due whether or not the guest collects (§ 326 (2) BGB). Whether Visa and Mastercard rules allow a no-show capture for a quick-service merchant is `unverified`.

**Small deposits.** A 1 € card deposit costs 0.27 € at Stripe, which is 85 % of the fee for prepaying the whole coffee. If it is credited at pickup and the guest pays the rest by card at the counter, the café pays two fees. A deposit only makes sense on percentage pricing or inside a pre-loaded balance. Legally, a deposit credited against the price is an ordinary advance payment. A forfeitable "no-show fee" on top of the price, written into standard terms, runs into § 309 Nr. 6 BGB, which voids penalties for non-collection ([§ 309 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__309.html)). Behaviour points the same way. In a field study at ten Haifa day-care centres (01.1998 to 06.1998), a fine for late pickup *increased* late pickups, and they stayed high after the fine was removed ([Gneezy & Rustichini 2000](https://Rady.ucsd.edu/_files/faculty-research/uri-gneezy/fine.pdf)). A token charge can turn an obligation into a price.

**Top-up balances.** A balance spreads the fixed fee across many coffees. A 20 € top-up at Stripe costs 0.55 €, or 0.11 € per 4 € coffee, and a 50 € top-up costs 1.00 €, or 0.08 € per coffee, against 0.31 € when each coffee is paid separately. LAP Coffee runs this model with its LAP Card, paid by Apple Pay, Google Pay, card or PayPal ([LAP FAQ](https://www.lap.coffee/faq)). Refunding unused balance does not return the original fee. Whether a balance is legal depends on who issues it and where it can be spent (next section).

**Bank rails.** SEPA Lastschrift is the wrong tool against no-shows. Stripe confirms a debit only after at least 6 business days, the guest can reverse it without giving a reason for 8 weeks, a failed debit costs 3.50 € and a dispute 15 € ([Stripe SEPA docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/sepa-debit); [§ 675x BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__675x.html)). A saboteur can type in any IBAN. Wero is structurally the best fit for prepayment against sabotage. Stripe supports it for German accounts, and it settles "in der Regel in weniger als 10 Sekunden", supports manual capture, has no dispute process at Stripe and has a 0.50 € minimum ([Stripe Wero docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/wero)). Its German price at Stripe is `unverified`, because the pricing page lists only "iDEAL | Wero 0.29 €"; Mollie charges German merchants 0.90 % + 0.25 €. Wero has been live in German e-commerce since 11.2025 (trade press, secondary) ([IT Finanzmagazin](https://www.it-finanzmagazin.de/epi-startet-wero-im-deutschen-onlinehandel-und-gewinnt-erste-grosse-haendler-236442/)), so it should sit alongside cards, not replace them. giropay ended on 31.12.2024 ([Sparkasse](https://www.sparkasse.de/unsere-loesungen/privatkunden/bezahlverfahren/paydirekt.html)).

**A service fee to cover the cost.** A flat fee of 0.32 € covers Stripe's card cost on a 4 € coffee, and 0.44 € covers it on a 12 € bowl. The fee must be the same for every payment method. A surcharge for SEPA or consumer cards is void under § 270a BGB, any method-specific fee is capped at actual cost under § 312a (4) BGB, and § 312m BGB catches disguised versions ([§ 270a BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__270a.html); [§ 312a BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312a.html); [§ 312m BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312m.html)). The BGH treated a "Servicegebühr" as an unlawful circumvention in X ZR 23/20 (24.08.2021; only a secondary summary was read) ([RWS Verlag](https://rws-verlag.de/aktuell/wirtschaftsrecht-aktuell/bgh-urteil-vom-24-august-2021-x-zr-2320-68967)). The order screen must show and highlight the total price (§ 3 PAngV), and OLG Frankfurt 6 U 29/19 (04.02.2021, secondary) required a mandatory fee to be included in the advertised price ([§ 3 PAngV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/pangv_2022/__3.html); [beck-aktuell](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/heute-im-recht/rechtsprechung/olg-frankfurt-am-main-fitnessstudio-muss-servicegebuehr-in-beworbenen-preis-einrechnen-2021-03-11)). The CJEU reportedly allowed a small-order surcharge to be shown separately in C-62/25 (26.03.2026), but only a law-firm summary was read, so this is `unverified` ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/deu/publication/update-commercial-04-2026)). **Open legal question:** if InfraMS charged guests a fee of its own, it would become a party on the guest's side of the contract, which makes the payment-licence problem below worse.

**When payment would pay for itself.** Two thresholds matter, and both are my own calculation from the cited figures. If the only aim is to stop waste, charging 0.31 € on every coffee costs more than the waste it prevents unless 25 % to 78 % of coffee orders go uncollected, because a wasted cappuccino costs 0.40 to 1.23 € in materials (see the operations section). For a 12 € bowl, the 0.43 € fee against a food cost of roughly 2.50 to 4.50 € (`unverified`) breaks even at 10 % to 17 % no-shows. If the café also counts the forfeited price of uncollected orders as income, prepayment comes out ahead once no-shows exceed the fee's share of the price: **about 7.8 % for coffee and 3.6 % for bowls**, before allowing for the counter card fees it replaces or for guests lost to checkout friction. The restaurant analogues of 1.30 % to 3.7 % sit below the coffee line and around the bowl line. Payment does not pay for itself on coffee at any plausible rate, and bowls are the first item where it might.

## 4. The platform must never hold the guests' money

The tempting shortcut, where the platform collects guests' payments and pays the cafés out, is the one option in this paper that carries criminal risk. Money accepted "im Namen des Zahlungsempfängers" and passed on is Finanztransfergeschäft under § 1 (1) Satz 2 Nr. 6 ZAG. BaFin names escrow services that collect on pooled accounts for internet trading portals as a covered case, and in that case the more specific acquiring offence applies ([BaFin Merkblatt ZAG, Stand 07.2024](https://www.bafin.de/SharedDocs/Veroeffentlichungen/DE/Merkblatt/mb_111222_zag.html)). Payment services need a BaFin licence under § 10 ZAG, and issuing e-money needs one under § 11 ZAG ([§ 10 ZAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/zag_2018/__10.html); [§ 11 ZAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/zag_2018/__11.html)). Operating without one carries **up to five years' imprisonment or a fine, and up to three years for negligence** ([§ 63 ZAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/zag_2018/__63.html)). The commercial-agent exception in § 2 (1) Nr. 2 ZAG almost certainly does not help. BaFin says online platforms that contribute to contracts "lediglich in automatisierter Weise" fall outside it, and that "[w]er auf beiden Seiten eines Rechtsgeschäfts steht, wie etwa eine Online-Handelsplattform, kann die Handelsvertreterausnahme nicht in Anspruch nehmen" ([BaFin Merkblatt ZAG](https://www.bafin.de/SharedDocs/Veroeffentlichungen/DE/Merkblatt/mb_111222_zag.html)). STAMM has terms with guests and with cafés, so it stands on both sides.

BaFin itself describes the legal route. A platform does not carry on Finanztransfer if it uses a licensed payment institution, but "die Partei des Grundgeschäfts ... [muss] jeweils einen eigenen Vertrag mit dem Zahlungsinstitut abschließen", and "[d]er Plattformbetreiber darf ... keine Einwirkungsmöglichkeit auf den Zahlungsfluss haben" ([BaFin Merkblatt ZAG](https://www.bafin.de/SharedDocs/Veroeffentlichungen/DE/Merkblatt/mb_111222_zag.html)). In Stripe terms, that means **Connect accounts under a `full` service agreement using direct charges**. Each café is the merchant of record and bears fees, refunds and chargebacks, and InfraMS takes an application fee ([Stripe service agreements](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/service-agreement-types); [Stripe Connect pricing](https://stripe.com/de/connect/pricing)). Mollie Connect's "Platforms" model works the same way, with each café holding its own Mollie account (search summary) ([Mollie Connect](https://docs.mollie.com/docs/connect-overview)). Avoid destination charges, "separate charges and transfers", `recipient` agreements and Mollie "Marketplaces", because in all of these the platform owns the payment or controls the routing. If Stripe prices the cafés directly, Connect costs InfraMS nothing extra. If InfraMS sets the pricing, Stripe charges 2 € per monthly active account, 0.25 % + 0.10 € per payout and 0.25 % for routing, which comes to about 23 € a month for a café with 3,600 € of monthly volume and 30 payouts (computed; whether the two 0.25 % charges overlap is `unverified`). **Open legal question:** whether the payout schedules that Express accounts let the platform set already count as "Einwirkungsmöglichkeit" is a grey zone.

Balances split cleanly along one line. **A balance issued by one café and spent only at that café is not e-money**, because e-money requires acceptance by someone other than the issuer ([§ 1 (2) ZAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/zag_2018/__1.html)). STAMM can run it as the café's technical provider under § 2 (1) Nr. 9 ZAG, as long as the top-up money flows straight into the café's own merchant account ([§ 2 ZAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/zag_2018/__2.html)). **A single STAMM balance spendable at several independent cafés is e-money** unless the limited-network exception applies. BaFin's conditions for that exception are a professional issuer legally separate from the cafés, a uniform brand, a limited area (for Frankfurt, postcode 60 and part of 65, extendable to all of 65 plus 61, 63 and 64), as a rule at most 250 € stored per instrument and 250 € topped up per calendar month, and a notification to BaFin once 12-month volume passes 1 Mio € ([BaFin Merkblatt ZAG](https://www.bafin.de/SharedDocs/Veroeffentlichungen/DE/Merkblatt/mb_111222_zag.html); [§ 2 ZAG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/zag_2018/__2.html)). The deciding risk is BaFin's sentence that "[d]er Betreiber eines Internet-Marktplatzes, auf dessen Plattform andere Anbieter Waren oder Dienstleistungen anbieten, kann die Bereichsausnahme allerdings weiterhin nicht in Anspruch nehmen". STAMM's multi-café front end looks very much like that. A cross-café balance therefore needs a licensed e-money partner or a legal opinion first, possibly with a BaFin enquiry. **Open legal question:** whether a refundable café balance counts as a deposit under the KWG is `unverified`. **Open tax question:** a mixed food-and-drink balance is likely a Mehrzweck-Gutschein taxed at redemption ([§ 3 UStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ustg_1980/__3.html)), which is an inference to confirm.

None of this changes soon. The EU payments overhaul (PSD3/PSR) reached provisional agreement on 27.11.2025 and ECON approval on 05.05.2026. The Parliament's Legislative Train lists it as "Close to adoption" (updated 25.10.2026) ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-an-economy-that-works-for-people/file-revision-of-eu-rules-on-payment-services)). A Council first-reading position was reportedly adopted on 28./29.10.2026 (search summary only), and nothing has appeared in the Official Journal. It is **not in force**. Industry sources expect publication around 12.2026 and application roughly 18 to 21 months later (secondary) ([Adyen](https://www.adyen.com/knowledge-hub/psd3)). Everything above is current ZAG 2018 law. Re-check the limited-network and commercial-agent wording once the text is published.

## 5. A no-show fee is valid on paper and worthless in practice

Paying at pickup does not take the order outside consumer law. §§ 312 ff. BGB apply wherever the consumer "sich ... zu der Zahlung eines Preises verpflichtet", whenever and however the payment happens ([§ 312 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312.html)). The button rule decides whether a contract exists at all. Unless the order button reads "zahlungspflichtig bestellen" or something equally unambiguous, "[e]in Vertrag ... kommt nur zustande, wenn der Unternehmer seine Pflicht aus Absatz 3 erfüllt" ([§ 312j BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312j.html)). A pay-at-pickup service with a plain "Bestellen" button is very likely running non-binding pre-orders, whether or not that was intended. LG Kiel held in 1998 that a mere table reservation only "diente ... der Anbahnung" of a later contract and denied lost profit ([LG Kiel, 8 S 160/97](https://lorenz.userweb.mwn.de/urteile/NJW98_2539.htm)). A binding order brings duties with it: say "pay at the counter only" when ordering starts (§ 312j (1) BGB), confirm receipt electronically and make the AGB retrievable ([§ 312i BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312i.html)), and handle the withdrawal right. That right is excluded for perishable goods and for food and drinks with a set time (§ 312g (2) Nr. 2 and Nr. 9 BGB), but not for coffee beans, bottled drinks or merch, so mixed baskets need care ([§ 312g BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312g.html)). Starbucks DE's terms include a full Widerrufsbelehrung with the perishable-goods exception, which suggests its German counsel treats app pre-orders as distance contracts (inference). **Open legal question:** binding or non-binding order, the button wording, and whether STAMM is an "Online-Marktplatz" with its own duties under § 312l BGB and Art. 246d EGBGB, or each café's technical provider ([§ 312l BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312l.html); [Art. 246d EGBGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgbeg/art_246d__1.html)).

With a binding order, a guest who misses the slot is in Annahmeverzug without any reminder, because the pickup time is a calendar date ([§ 296 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__296.html)). The café keeps its price claim for a drink that spoils, minus what it saves ([§ 326 (2) BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__326.html)), and can recover extra costs ([§ 304 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__304.html)). Standard terms cannot add a penalty on top. **A Vertragsstrafe for non-collection is void per se (§ 309 Nr. 6 BGB).** A lump-sum damages clause survives only if it stays within the typical damage *and* expressly lets the guest prove a lower or zero loss (§ 309 Nr. 5 BGB) ([§ 309 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__309.html)). VAT is not a loss: AG München (21.10.2024, 191 C 19029/24, not final) awarded **2,508.64 € net of the 2,775 € claimed** for a set-menu party that never came ([AG München, PM 39/2024](https://www.justiz.bayern.de/gerichte-und-behoerden/amtsgerichte/muenchen/presse/2024/39.php)). A café lump sum would therefore have to stay at or below the net price of the uncollected items, and a 5 € fee on a 3.50 € espresso would very likely fail (inference). The BGH upheld 10 % and 15 % lump sums for non-acceptance of cars (VIII ZR 123/09, VIII ZR 165/11; summaries only), which is an analogy at best ([Betriebs-Berater](https://betriebs-berater.ruw.de/wirtschaftsrecht/urteile/Schadenspauschalierungsklausel-in-AGB-eines-Kraftsfahrzeughaendlers-18856)). No German decision on click-and-collect no-shows, or on a restaurant no-show clause in AGB, was found. Verbraucherzentrale Niedersachsen (Stand 22.07.2026) says case law is not uniform and that restaurants "müssen belegen, dass ihnen ein nachweisbarer Schaden entstanden ist" ([VZ Niedersachsen](https://www.verbraucherzentrale-niedersachsen.de/themen/kaufen-reklamieren/produkte-kaufen/ausfallgebuehr-no-show-gebuehr-was-ist-das-muessen-sie-sie-bezahlen)). Any extra charge must also be agreed expressly, never through a pre-ticked box (§ 312a (3) BGB).

Enforcement kills the idea before the law does. To claim 4 €, the café pays at least 38 € to apply for a Mahnbescheid, or a 120 € court fee for a lawsuit on any claim up to 500 € ([GKG Anlage 1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gkg_2004/anlage_1.html); [§ 34 GKG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gkg_2004/__34.html)). A Mahnbescheid needs a name and a postal address, and STAMM holds only an email address and a Wallet pass serial. Without money taken up front, the claim exists on paper only. The levers that work in practice are not making the item yet and not letting the same card order again.

## 6. A web app cannot geofence, but a tap at the counter can

Geofencing has a short answer: a web app like STAMM cannot do it without a native app. The W3C Geolocation spec delivers positions only to visible, fully active pages ("Position updates are exclusively for fully-active visible documents") ([W3C Geolocation, draft dated 18.11.2026](https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/)). A WebKit contributor wrote that running websites in the background with the screen off "is not something WebKit supports on iOS" (2019, the most authoritative statement found) ([WebKit mailing list](https://lists.webkit.org/pipermail/webkit-unassigned/2019-June/875862.html)). The spec gives a page no way to tell a faked position from a real one. iOS has a native "simulated by software" flag, but one developer reported it returned `false` against a spoofing tool on iOS 18.0, and a web app cannot read it anyway ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/803179)). Web Push works only for home-screen web apps on iOS 16.4 and later, and permission must follow a tap ([WebKit blog, 16.02.2023](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)). When Apple reversed its plan to remove home-screen web apps in the EU (01.03.2024), it had earlier cited very low use of them ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/03/01/apple-walks-back-decision-to-disable-eu-web-apps/)). Expect little reach.

Apple Wallet does not fill the gap. A pass can list up to 10 `locations`, but iOS uses them on the phone to show the pass on the lock screen ([Apple Pass docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletpasses/pass); [Apple lock-screen docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletpasses/showing-a-pass-on-the-lock-screen)). Nothing reaches STAMM's server, and no server callback is documented (`unverified`, very likely none). Developers report undocumented lock-screen changes in iOS 18 ([Apple forums, 11.2024](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/769909)). Wallet Orders models pickup well, with the statuses `open`, `processing`, `readyForPickup`, `pickedUp`, `issue` and `cancelled`, a `pickupAt` start and a `pickupWindowDuration`, but it has no location trigger ([Apple PickupFulfillment](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletorders/pickupfulfillment)). Both remain useful as one-way nudges. Setting the pass's location to the café while an order is open puts a reminder on the honest guest's lock screen, and status pushes tell the guest the order is ready.

The chains that cut wait and waste use the same lever: hold the order and start it on an arrival signal. McDonald's "Ready on Arrival" (US pilot from 03.2023) starts assembly when a geofence shows the guest is about three minutes away ([Restaurant Technology News](https://restauranttechnologynews.com/2023/03/mcdonalds-tests-new-technology-to-reduce-waiting-times-for-mobile-orders/)), and McDonald's UK does the same with its Order Ahead feature ([McDonald's UK](https://www.mcdonalds.com/gb/en-gb/newsroom/article/jump-the-quee-mcdonalds-order-ahead.html)). Domino's Carside (US, 06.2020) is limited to prepaid orders, and the guest taps "I'm here" or texts "HERE" ([Domino's](https://media.dominos.com/stories/carside-delivery/)). Chipotle's carside test used a manual tap plus car details ([Retail Systems](https://retailsystems.org/carside-pickup-chipotle-expands-to-170-panera-geofencing/)). None of them published measured outcomes. One analysis notes that an arrival signal does not create bar capacity during a rush (opinion) ([Lean Blog, 03.2026](https://www.leanblog.org/2026/03/starbucks-mobile-order-timing-problem-chick-fil-a-solved/)).

For STAMM, the usable signals rank as follows. A "Bin unterwegs" or "Bin da" button, on the order page, in the confirmation mail or on the back of the pass, costs nothing to install, but it is a claim, not proof. Adding a one-shot location check while the page is open stops lazy trolls tapping from home, but it adds a permission prompt, fails indoors, and anyone motivated can fake it. **A rotating code shown only on the counter screen, which the guest scans or types in to say "Bin da", proves physical presence.** That is a design inference that no source has tested in a café. A barista scanning the pass or the Wallet order barcode at handoff confirms collection, not arrival. In Wallet Orders terms, keep an order `open` until the guest's signal, set `processing` on the signal, `readyForPickup` when the barista marks it ready, then `pickedUp` or `cancelled` for "nicht abgeholt", with `pickupWindowDuration` set to a 30-minute window. The counter view should show "neu", "unterwegs" and "da" as separate lanes, and baristas make drinks only from the confirmed lanes.

Location has a legal cost. Reading the phone's position is access to the terminal device under § 25 TDDDG and needs consent unless it is strictly necessary for a service the user "ausdrücklich gewünscht" has ([§ 25 TDDDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ttdsg/__25.html)). The data protection authorities' guidance takes a per-function view of what the user actually asked for ([DSK OH Digitale Dienste v1.2, 11.2024](https://datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH_Digitale_Dienste.pdf)). Canada's privacy commissioners found on 01.06.2022 that the Tim Hortons app had tracked users' location **more than 2,700 times in under five months**, including in the background ([Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada](https://priv.gc.ca/en/opc-news/news-and-announcements/2022/nr-c_220601/)). The safe pattern is an opt-in toggle per order that stores only "within X m of the café" (not coordinates), deletes it when the order closes, and does not feed it into the strike system without a separately disclosed purpose. **Open legal question:** whether that toggle counts as "strictly necessary" or needs a TDDDG consent, and whether location hints in the pass, evaluated only on the phone, trigger § 25 for STAMM at all (`unverified`).

## 7. A wasted cappuccino costs under 1.25 €; the bar time and the bowl cost more

A café guide (published 11.08.2026) breaks a 3.50 € cappuccino into **0.40 € of materials**: 8 g of beans for 0.24 € and 0.12 l of milk for 0.14 €, cup not included ([business-on.de](https://www.business-on.de/cafe-eroeffnen-kosten.html)). A range built from cited prices runs from about 0.32 to 0.40 € at the low end to about 1.10 to 1.23 € at the high end. The inputs are bean prices of 27.90 to 47.60 €/kg gross ([Kaffeezentrale](https://www.kaffeezentrale.de/julius-meinl-espresso-arabica-1-kg-bohne-utz); [Aromatico](https://www.aromatico.de/en/p/hoppenworth-ploch-brazil-espresso-dark)), whole milk at 0.95 €/l ([Lidl](https://unternehmen.lidl.de/content/download/146824/4061214?version=1)), barista milk at 1.06 €/l at Selgros ([prospektangebote](https://www.prospektangebote.de/angebote/barista-milch)) and cups at 0.02 to 0.04 € ([wisefood](https://www.wisefood.eu/en/collections/kaffeebecher-to-go.oembed?page=2)). The 16 to 20 g double dose, the milk wastage and the lid price are assumptions (`unverified`), and every café's recipes differ. Against an average cappuccino price of 3.80 € (1,232 cafés in ten cities, 06.2026, snippet only) ([ms-aktuell](https://ms-aktuell.de/welt/kaffeepreise-cafes-19-09-2026/)), materials are roughly 10 % to 30 % of the price, and coffee costs are rising: Destatis reported coffee products up 5.6 % year on year in 06.2026 ([Destatis](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/07/PD26_243_611.html)). A 10 to 15 € bowl is the real exposure. At the 25 % to 30 % food cost one aggregator suggests, it costs 2.50 to 4.50 € to make (`unverified`) ([beancount.io](https://beancount.io/de/blog/2026/01/25/coffee-shop-bookkeeping-complete-financial-guide)), and it cannot be resold once plated.

At peak, the bigger loss is bar time, estimated at 1 to 2 minutes per drink (`unverified`). Starbucks admitted in its Q1 FY2017 earnings call that mobile orders were jamming the hand-off counter in its busiest stores and that some walk-in customers left ([Fortune, 27.01.2017](https://fortune.com/2017/01/27/starbucks-mobile-orders-slowdown/)). Mobile orders have since reached 31 % to 33 % of US company-operated Starbucks transactions (FY25 to Q3 FY26, investor dashboards, snippet-level) ([Starbucks IR](https://s203.q4cdn.com/326826266/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/Q3-FY26-Digital-IR-Dashboard.pdf)). In OpenTable's UK operator survey (reported 06.10.2026), 60 % said no-shows made them turn away other bookings, 48 % cited wasted food and 41 % said no-shows make staffing harder to plan ([Restaurant Online](https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2026/10/06/no-show-rates-fall-year-on-year-report-finds/)).

That cost structure sets prep timing by item class. Batch brew, cold drinks, bottled drinks, pastries and packaged goods can be set aside at order time, because the marginal cost of a no-show is close to zero. A pastry goes back into the display, a bottle back into the fridge, and batch brew is held anyway: SCA Standard 310-2021 expects 80 to 85 °C over the first 30 minutes of holding ([SCA](https://sca.coffee/s/SCA_Standard_310-2021.pdf)), and in one sensory study an industry panel showed no preference between fresh and held coffee (snippet) ([PubMed 36524730](https://www.pubmed.gov/36524730/)). **Espresso-milk drinks and bowls should start on the guest's arrival signal** for anyone without a pickup record. Guests with a clean history can have them started at the pickup time minus prep time. An all-day café should be able to switch between "only on arrival" at peak and earlier starts off-peak.

Arrival-based starts make hold time a minor question, because only made-ahead orders for trusted guests ever wait. Starbucks has no official hold time. Baristas reported binning uncollected orders after 20 to 25 minutes (anecdotal), and a Starbucks spokesperson said baristas **remake** a drink that has degraded, or one that was already thrown away when the guest arrives ([The Takeout, 28.04.2022](https://thetakeout.com/how-long-will-starbucks-wait-before-tossing-your-mobile-1848853987)). Other baristas reported giving unclaimed drinks away free ([Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/2199987/what-starbucks-does-mobile-orders-never-picked-up/)). A workable house rule for a small café is to hold milk drinks 15 to 20 minutes past the slot (inference from those anecdotes), then sell them at a discount to the next walk-in or give them to staff or a regular, put pastries and bottles back on sale, and remake for a late guest so a binned drink never decides whether that guest comes back. Too Good To Go (about 26,000 partners and more than 19 million users in Germany, 2026) suits end-of-day pastries and bowl components, not single hot drinks ([Lebensmittelpraxis](https://lebensmittelpraxis.de/handel-aktuell/49452-lebensmittelverschwendung-denns-biomarkt-und-too-good-to-go-verkaufen-mehr-als-zwei-millionen-ueberraschungstueten.html)). The rule should be written down. The Starbucks accounts show that most friction comes from staff deciding case by case.

## 8. A barista's stamp beats any identity check money can buy

Email, magic links, passkeys and Wallet passes are all free for an attacker to create in bulk. Only two credentials have real cost for a saboteur: a phone number, and a barista's stamp.

| Option | Friction for an honest guest | Cost for an attacker to get around it | Running cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email only (today) | None | None: aliases, catch-all and disposable domains | None |
| Magic link before the first order | One inbox round-trip | Low | One mail |
| Disposable-domain blocklist plus alias normalisation | None | Low: own domain | None |
| SMS one-time code, +49 numbers only | One code per new card (20-40 s, `unverified`) | Medium: a SIM or virtual number per identity | Twilio Verify USD 0.05 per verification plus a per-message channel fee (DE amount `unverified`); SMS list price USD 0.112 per segment |
| Passkey | Low on iOS 16+ | None: unlimited accounts | None |
| Wallet pass possession | None | None if cards are self-serve; `sharingProhibited` only hides the Share button | None |
| Barista stamp at the till | First visit cannot pre-order | High: a paid, in-person visit per card per café, seen by staff | None |

*Sources: [Twilio SMS pricing DE](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/de) (fetched 18.11.2026), [Twilio Verify pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/user-authentication-identity/pricing/verify) (snippet), [Apple Pass docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletpasses/pass), [disposable-email-domains list](https://github.com/disposable-email-domains/disposable-email-domains).*

SMS adds a new attack. In SMS pumping, bots submit premium-rate or attacker-controlled numbers to the code form, and the site pays the termination fees ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/cybersecurity/feature/SMS-pumping-attacks-and-how-to-mitigate-them)). If SMS is ever added, it needs to sit behind a bot check, accept only +49 numbers, block VoIP and premium lines, and limit codes per IP. It also needs an SMS provider as a data processor. The barista's stamp is different in kind. It is a server-side event created by a trusted person who saw the guest, not something the guest presents, and no email, phone or passkey scheme proves presence the same way.

A handful of simple rules already form a sound ladder, and the sources support each rung. Unlocking pre-ordering after a first till stamp blocks all remote, anonymous sabotage, much as eBay's limits block new sellers. One no-show switching pre-ordering off until the next till stamp mirrors the rider-reported Zomato practice and Uber's carried-over balance, and it costs an honest guest who forgot only one normal purchase at the till. One open order per card stops floods from a single card. A value cap that rises with history stops big-ticket pranks. The 30-minute window limits how long a stale order clogs the counter. **Combined with arrival-based starts, these rules mean a remote attacker cannot make a café produce a single drink.** Each sabotaged drink requires a physical visit and a paid stamp first, and the card is dead after one no-show. The real trade-off is that first-time guests cannot pre-order. If pre-ordering is meant to win new guests, there are two alternatives: let unstamped cards order but start their items only after the in-café "Bin da" code, or add the SMS code for first orders.

| Level | How a card gets there | What it can pre-order | When the café starts it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | New card, no till stamp at this café | Nothing (strict rule), or drinks and pastries started only after the in-café "Bin da" code | Only on in-café code, if allowed at all |
| 1 | At least one barista stamp at this café, no open strike | One open order, drinks and pastries only | On the "Bin unterwegs" or "Bin da" tap |
| 2 | Several collected pre-orders, no no-show (for example 3) | Bowls and larger orders | At pickup time minus prep time |
| Paused | One "nicht abgeholt" | Nothing until the next till stamp or an owner reset | n/a |

No platform publishes its own thresholds, so the numbers in this ladder are business choices, not sourced figures. OpenTable allows four no-shows in twelve months, and TheFork warns before it suspends, so a one-strike rule is stricter than either. It stays reasonable because re-arming takes one ordinary purchase.

Scripted floods need cheaper controls. Cloudflare Turnstile is free and usually invisible (plan details from a search snippet; request caps `unverified`) ([Cloudflare Turnstile plans](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/plans)). It stops scripts but not a human troll, so it belongs in front of card creation and the first order, not every order from a trusted card. Disposable-domain lists and Gmail dot-and-plus normalisation make throwaway identities slower to produce. The control aimed squarely at sabotage is a **per-café velocity limit with a one-tap "Vorbestellung pausieren" switch on the counter view**. TeamKompass asked Lieferando to flag "spam orders sent to one restaurant" and to limit the email addresses per customer ([MeinMMO, 15.03.2024](https://mein-mmo.de/en/essensraids-problem-auf-twitch,1103034)), and a WooCommerce fraud guide recommends holding orders for review during fraud waves (snippet) ([wpslash](https://www.wpslash.com/?p=4849)). No source has tested the pause switch in a café. It is a design suggestion.

## 9. Switching off a no-show's pre-ordering is lawful if it is disclosed and contestable

Refusing *future* pre-orders needs no special justification. The BGH holds that whoever controls the premises may "in der Regel frei darüber ... entscheiden, wem er den Zutritt gestattet", without objective reasons. Wrecking a contract already concluded, however, "bedarf der Rechtfertigung durch besonders gewichtige Sachgründe" ([BGH V ZR 115/11, 09.03.2012](https://lorenz.userweb.mwn.de/urteile/vzr115_11.htm)). The main limit is the AGG: a rule must not work as a proxy for race, ethnic origin, sex, religion, disability, age or sexual identity in mass transactions ([§ 19 AGG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/agg/__19.html)). A reset route ("ich hatte einen Notfall") lowers the risk of indirect disability discrimination. **Never auto-cancel an order that has already been accepted because of a past strike.** That needs weighty reasons, and the GDPR guidance treats "cancellation of a contract" as a legal effect ([WP251rev.01](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/redirection/document/49826)).

The data side is manageable. A no-show history kept to restrict future orders is best based on legitimate interest under Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR ([GDPR, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)). The Saxon data protection authority found "keinen Datenschutzverstoß" in an online club's internal email blacklist that enforced a ban. Its reasons were that the ban was legitimate, the terms reserved it, the privacy notice disclosed it and the list did not leave the company. It accepted indefinite storage only after "groben und/oder mehrfachen Verstößen" ([Sächsischer Datenschutzbeauftragter, Tätigkeitsbericht 2022, pp. 70-71](https://www.datenschutz.sachsen.de/download/taetigkeitsberichte/Taetigkeitsbericht_Datenschutz_2022.pdf)). An automatic one-strike switch-off is "solely automated" under Art. 22 GDPR. It probably falls below the "similarly significant" bar, which the guidance ties to things like credit, health care, jobs and education ([WP251rev.01](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/redirection/document/49826)). The CJEU, however, read "decision" broadly in the SCHUFA case (C-634/21, 07.12.2023, secondary) ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/de/insights/doppelpack-fuer-die-schufa-vor-dem-eugh-schufa-score-unterliegt-art-22-dsgvo-informationen)). The safe design has four parts. First, a clear rule in the AGB before the first order. Second, an automatic notice to the guest stating the reason. Third, a one-tap contest that the café owner reviews and can actually overturn (WP251 rejects "token" human review). Fourth, a privacy-notice paragraph on the logic, consequences, legal basis, retention period and contest route, as Art. 13 (2) (f) GDPR requires. **Open legal question:** if the loyalty terms make pre-ordering a contractual right, switching it off may count as a legal effect under Art. 22.

No statute fixes how long a strike may be kept. A single no-show is neither grave nor repeated, so it should expire. A 6 to 12 month window is a business choice with no legal source. A ban record can be kept while the ban is active, and any record tied to an actual money claim no longer than the three-year limitation period ([§ 195 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__195.html)). For comparison, the transport company BOGESTRA keeps ban data for 3 years ([BOGESTRA](https://www.bogestra.de/datenschutz/hausverbot)). **Open question for counsel and the data protection officer:** set the periods.

Sharing strikes across cafés is a different legal animal. Per-café strike lists, run on each café's instructions, fit a processor set-up under Art. 28 GDPR with a data processing agreement per café. Once STAMM uses café A's data to protect café B, STAMM decides the purpose, and the cafés and STAMM are most likely joint controllers under Art. 26. That requires an arrangement whose essence guests can see, and the CJEU does not require a formal agreement for joint controllership to arise (C-683/21, 05.12.2023, secondary) ([GvW](https://www.gvw.com/aktuelles/blog/detail/bussgelder-und-gemeinsame-verantwortlichkeit-vor-dem-eugh-neues-zum-datenschutz-aus-luxemburg)). The Saxon decision leaned on the list staying internal, so a shared list loses that support. No German authority guidance on shared no-show lists was found. Default to per-café strikes. If a shared flag is ever wanted, limit it to a yes/no "Vorbestellung pausiert" set only after repeated no-shows, with hashed identifiers and prominent disclosure. **Open question for counsel and the data protection officer:** decide this, including whether a DPIA is needed (`unverified`).

The identifier used for blocking matters too. From lowest to highest legal risk: the Wallet pass serial STAMM issued (server-side, no new device access), then the verified email STAMM already collects, then a phone number (new data that needs its own purpose), then a device fingerprint. A fingerprint counts as access to the device under § 25 TDDDG, and the DSK warns that "[e]ine pauschale Klassifizierung als ‚Sicherheitscookie' ist nicht ausreichend" ([DSK OH Digitale Dienste](https://datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/oh/OH_Digitale_Dienste.pdf)). Blocking by Wallet serial combined with the till-stamp unlock gives strong protection without collecting anything new.

## 10. Specific euro costs and a named barista beat fines

The best controlled evidence on getting people to show up comes from healthcare and public services. Two cheap message changes work. In an NHS trial at Barts Health (11.2013 to 01.2014), adding "Not attending costs NHS £160 approx." to the reminder **cut missed appointments from 11.1 % to 8.4 %** (n = 2,445 in that arm), while a social-norm message (10.0 %) was not significant. In the follow-up trial (03.2014 to 05.2014), a vague "wastes NHS money" message (9.9 %) and an empathy appeal (10.7 %) both did worse than the specific figure (8.2 %) ([Hallsworth et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4569397)). For a café, that means a concrete euro figure taken from its own invoices, not the word "Verschwendung". In OpenTable's UK diner survey (10.2026), 48 % said knowing the cost of a no-show would make them more likely to turn up or cancel ([Restaurant Online](https://www.restaurantonline.co.uk/Article/2026/10/06/no-show-rates-fall-year-on-year-report-finds/)).

A personal, reciprocal message from a named person did even better in a different setting. At Bedford Jobcentre (05.2013 to 12.2013, n = 1,224), "I've booked you a place. Good luck, [Adviser name]" raised attendance at optional recruitment events **from 10.5 % to 26.8 %** ([Sanders & Kirkman 2019](https://journal-bpa.org/index.php/jbpa/article/view/24)). That study measured turnout to an optional event, not no-shows, and the same paper cites evidence that named reminders work best where a relationship already exists. That fits a regular's café. A confirmation such as "[Barista-Name] hat deinen Flat White für 09:15 eingeplant. Bis gleich!" uses both effects. Letting the guest pick an exact pickup time also helps. Prompting people to write down the date *and* time of a planned flu shot raised vaccination by 4.2 points over a 33.1 % baseline, while a date-only prompt did not reach significance ([Milkman et al. 2011](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/using-implementation-intentions-prompts-enhance-influenza-vaccination-rates)). Reminders help only modestly. A Cochrane review found text reminders raised attendance with a risk ratio of 1.14 ([Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD007458_mobile-phone-messaging-reminders-attendance-healthcare-appointments)), and Resos saw 16 % fewer no-shows with a delivered SMS (observational) but near-zero effect from confirmation prompts ([Resos](https://resos.com/restaurant-no-show-rate/)). For orders placed 10 to 30 minutes ahead, a reminder adds little.

The stamp card itself can raise the stakes. Car-wash cards with two stamps pre-filled were completed by 34 % of customers against 19 % for empty cards needing the same purchases (04.2004; secondary summaries) ([Coglode](https://www.coglode.com/nuggets/endowed-progress-effect)). In a real café coffee programme, purchases sped up by about 20 % as members neared the free coffee ([Kivetz et al. 2006](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1200/goalgradient.pdf)). In a Penn trial, money people already held and could lose beat an equal gain (45 % vs 30 % of days meeting a step goal) ([Penn Medicine, 2016](https://www.pennmedicine.org/news/news-releases/2016/february/to-encourage-physical-activity)). A **"pending stamp"** that appears in the Wallet card as reserved when the guest orders and becomes final at pickup borrows these effects without a fee. It is untested: no study was found on stamps at risk as a no-show deterrent. The lever is also weakest where sabotage risk is highest, because a fresh throwaway card has nothing to lose, so it complements the trust ladder rather than replacing it. Social-norm copy ("97 % holen ab") should wait until the café has real numbers to quote.

## 13. Conclusion

The research changes the question. "How do we make no-shows pay?" has no good answer for a 4 € coffee: the fees are too high, the law voids penalties, and the courts cost more than the claim. "How do we make no-shows free?" has a cheap one. If nothing is made before the guest signals, a fake order becomes an expired row in Convex instead of a drink down the sink. A barista's stamp is also a presence credential that no SMS code, card check or passkey can match. Payment's measured power is mostly selection, getting reliable guests to self-identify. A trust ladder does that selection without fees and without turning InfraMS into a regulated payment business.

The money question does not disappear; it moves to a specific item and a measurable threshold. Bowls at busy cafés are where prepayment first earns its fee, and only through the café's own merchant account. The pilot's most valuable output is therefore a number nobody has published: the real "nicht abgeholt" rate for pay-at-pickup café orders, broken down by item class and trust level. That number, not chain practice or vendor deposit statistics, should decide when STAMM adds payment and for what.
